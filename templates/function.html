BACK TO PANIC AT THE PAN

ME218a TEAM 12 / ONE-PAGE DESCRIPTION OF FUNCTION

The function story.

Panic at the Pan is a tabletop cooking arcade game in which a player selects ingredients, controls simulated heat with a hovering hand, and stirs a dish with a joystick. The finished machine combines a laser-cut enclosure, a 3D-printed pan and spatula, displays, cooking audio and an edible reward.

The green Start button begins the game. The red Stop button ends play when stopping is allowed. Each button illuminates only when its action is available. Two chained LED matrix display assemblies communicate the dish being cooked, time and score.

Five ingredient switches let the player add food. Switching an ingredient on causes an icon to appear on the e-paper screen in the middle of the pan. The switches are Protein, Carb, Vegetables, Spice and Oil. Each ingredient counts once; the newest sets the heat target.

An ultrasonic sensor measures the distance of the player’s hand above the heat-control area. Moving closer requests more heat; moving farther away requests less. The LCD displays a varying heat bar. A speaker plays a simple background song and a frying texture that changes with hand position, giving both visual and audible feedback.

The joystick controls the back-and-forth sweep of a small servo-driven, 3D-printed spatula inside the pan. This visible motion is separate from the reward mechanism and replaced the earlier circular, homed-stepper concept.

Two PIC32MX170F256B controllers divide the work. One handles the LCD, ultrasonic sensor and speaker. The other runs the main game state machine and the remaining functions. PIC2 also reads the input expander and drives button lamps. UART2 carries CRC-protected, acknowledged switch events, range telemetry and repeated display/audio commands between the two controllers.

The integrated code starts a 60-second countdown after the dish introduction, enables Stop/Finish at 30 seconds, and returns to Welcome after 20 seconds without activity. A normal ending with at least 50 points releases a reward. The team reports that the complete machine worked at grading and received full credit on all graded items.

The reward was individual Gushers pieces. The dispenser could not fit a complete pouch, so the team changed the reward format to fit its mechanism. The final report still needs the associated handling and approval details.
